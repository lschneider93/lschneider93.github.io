The biostatistician's disclaimer: FAERS is a spontaneous reporting system. Reports are unverified, causality is not established, and reporting rates are shaped by media attention, litigation, and how long a drug has been on the market. Counts here are not incidence rates; a drug with more reports is not necessarily less safe. Source: openFDA drug adverse events.

How this dashboard works (for the technically curious)

No backend, no build step. The page fetches JSON from data.cdc.gov/resource/rdmq-nq56.json (Socrata/SODA query parameters; 3 requests) and api.fda.gov/drug/event.json (count queries only, 11 per drug lookup; no individual reports are downloaded). Responses are cached in localStorage with a TTL of 12–24 hours; if a live fetch fails, the page falls back to cached data and says so. Charts are Plotly.js. Panels render lazily on first activation because Plotly mis-sizes charts drawn into hidden elements, the kind of detail you learn by shipping.

The data-quality cards, raw-row tables, and missingness chart are computed in your browser from the same responses that draw the charts, so what you can verify is exactly what you are shown. The data dictionaries list every column the sources publish, including the ones this page ignores.

Source: js/ on GitHub. cdc.js and openfda.js are small fetch/transform modules; table.js renders the sortable tables; dashboard.js orchestrates.
