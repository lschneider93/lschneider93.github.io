Public health, fetched live.

This page pulls current data from two federal APIs directly in your browser — CDC emergency-department visit trends for respiratory illness, and FDA drug adverse-event reports. There's no server behind it: just JavaScript, two public APIs, a cache, and a statistician's caveats where they belong.

The biostatistician's disclaimer: FAERS is a spontaneous reporting system. Reports are unverified, causality is not established, and reporting rates are shaped by media attention, litigation, and how long a drug has been on the market. Counts here are not incidence rates — a drug with more reports is not necessarily less safe. Source: openFDA drug adverse events.

How this dashboard works (for the technically curious)

No backend, no build step. The page fetches JSON from data.cdc.gov/resource/rdmq-nq56.json (Socrata/SODA query parameters) and api.fda.gov/drug/event.json (count queries only — no individual reports are downloaded). Responses are cached in localStorage with a TTL of 12–24 hours; if a live fetch fails, the page falls back to cached data and says so. Charts are Plotly.js. Panels render lazily on first activation, because Plotly mis-sizes charts drawn into hidden elements — the kind of detail you learn by shipping.

Source: js/ on GitHub — cdc.js and openfda.js are small fetch/transform modules; dashboard.js orchestrates.
