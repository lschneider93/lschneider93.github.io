Read this like a hydrologist: nearly every live reading is provisional and can be revised after review. Percentiles compare a gage only with itself, and the number of years behind them varies by site and by day (Feb 29 has a quarter of the data other days have). Many Utah gages sit below dams and diversions, so "much below normal" can mean an irrigation schedule, not a drought. Extremes here are often real: the Great Salt Lake genuinely reaches triple the salinity of seawater, and the Virgin River genuinely hits 3,960 turbidity units in a flash flood.

How this dashboard works (for the technically curious)

No backend, no build step. The page calls three USGS water services: iv/ for the latest readings statewide (one request), stat/ for per-calendar-day percentile tables (the service caps a request at 10 sites, so ranking the whole state takes about 17 batched requests), and dv/ for daily history (featured gage and the Great Salt Lake). All are CORS-open and keyless. Responses are transformed first and then cached in localStorage, because the raw statewide payload is about a megabyte and the quota is five.

The percentile math is done in your browser: today's flow is placed on the day's p05 to p95 anchors by piecewise linear interpolation, and clamped to "below the 5th" or "above the 95th" outside them. The feed quirks the quality card reports (stale sensors, sentinel values, multi-sensor rigs) were found by exploratory analysis before this page was built, and the filters encode what that analysis found.

Source: js/ on GitHub. usgs.js is the fetch/transform module; water.js orchestrates; table.js renders the sortable tables.
