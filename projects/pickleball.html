Case Study · Computer Vision · Jan 2026 – Present

What can a match video reveal about a player's decisions?

And how can it help them improve? A computer vision pipeline that turns recorded gameplay into data on shot selection and play.

Question
Players record their games but have no simple way to review their shot choices. Can the footage from one camera be turned into data a player can learn from?
What I did
Built a computer vision pipeline that finds the ball, the players, and the court in every frame, marks each hit and bounce, places them on a court map in feet, and names serves and returns. Every model is scored against answers I label by hand, and only changes that measurably improve get kept.
What it shows
It runs end to end on a single rally from a fixed camera and produces a court map and a shot list. Shot naming beyond serves and returns, and player identity across rallies, are not yet reliable. One camera cannot measure ball height.
Why it matters
Coaching feedback today depends on a coach being there. A measured record of a session makes review possible for anyone with a phone on a tripod.

What it does today, and what it is meant to become

Today

A measured rally

Give it one rally clip from a fixed camera. It finds the ball, the players, and the court; marks every hit and bounce; places each one on a court map in feet; and names the serve and the return. Every model is scored against hand-labeled answers.

Where it falls short:

  • One camera cannot measure ball height, so lobs and dinks rely on rough guesses.
  • Corner and side camera angles are less accurate than a view from behind the baseline.
  • Player identity across rallies is not yet reliable.
  • Shot names beyond serve and return are not yet checked against hand labels.
  • A whole session is not yet split into games automatically.
Intended

A coaching tool

Record a session, upload it, and get back what a coach would tell you: which shots you chose at each moment, the patterns that won and lost rallies, and what to practice next. The steps below are the path from here to there.

Three models, one video

The pipeline opens the video once. Three models look at every frame, and each one finds one thing.

How a video gets broken down

Five steps, in order. Each one hands its results to the next.

01

Split the match into games

A recording of a session can hold several games. The first step finds where each game starts and ends.

Next

Next: build the game splitter. Today clips are cut by hand.

02

Split each game into rallies

A rally starts with a serve and ends when the ball is dead. The pipeline marks the start and end of every rally.

Working

Next: handle broadcast replays, so a replayed rally is not counted twice.

03

Find every ball event

A ball event is a moment the ball is hit by a paddle or hits the ground. The pipeline follows the ball's path and flags each one.

Working

Next: catch hits hidden behind a player, which is where most misses happen.

04

Locate the players, ball, and court at each event

At each event the pipeline records where the ball landed and where every player stood, in feet on the court map.

In progress

Next: measure how far off the player positions are from hand-clicked truth, and tighten the court fit from corner camera angles.

05

Name each shot and find the shots that mattered

Each shot gets a name (serve, return, drive, drop, dink, lob). Then the pipeline looks for the shots that helped a team win or lose the rally.

Next

Next: label a set of shots by hand so the shot names can be checked, then define what makes a shot influential.

Working means it runs on all my test clips and is checked against hand-labeled answers. In progress means it runs but the numbers are not where I want them. Next means it is planned.

Tools, in plain words

YOLO11 detectionA model trained on thousands of labeled frames to draw a box around one kind of thing, like the ball. Three of them run here: ball, players, court.
Pose keypointsA model that marks joints on a person (feet, knees, hips, shoulders, wrists), so you can measure a stance or a swing.
One decode passReading a video is slow. The video is opened once and all three models look at each frame as it goes by.
Kalman filterThe ball is sometimes missed for a few frames. The filter predicts where the ball should be next and smooths the path, so the trail stays connected.
ByteTrackKeeps the same number on the same player from frame to frame, so Player 1 stays Player 1.
HomographyThe math that maps the slanted court in the picture onto a flat court map, so every position is in real feet instead of pixels.
Scene cutsBroadcast footage switches cameras. This spots each switch so the court map is rebuilt for the new angle.
Cached stagesEach step saves its results. Changing a later step never re-runs the slow model steps.
Hand-labeled truthI click the real answers on frames (where the ball is, where the court corners are, when a hit happened), then score how often the models agree with me.

What is next

  • Put the per-rally stats on a simple dashboard a player can read.
  • Keep growing the hand-labeled truth sets, because every model improvement is checked against them.
