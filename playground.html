Words used on this page

  • Kitchen: the 7 ft strip on each side of the net where you may not volley (hit the ball out of the air).
  • Deep, mid, shallow: how far back the ball landed. A deep serve lands in the last 3 ft before the baseline. A deep return lands 19 ft or more past the net.
  • Lanes: a serve must land in one 10 ft wide service court. I split it in three, named from the receiver: sideline, body, middle. Returns can land anywhere, so they use thirds of the full court.
  • Third shot: the serving team's first shot after the return. A drop is a soft ball into the kitchen. A drive is a hard, flat one.
  • Stacking: partners line up so each keeps a favorite side. In this match the receiving pair has to switch sides after the return whenever its own score is odd. That is read from the score, not checked on video.
How this data was made

I labelled the match shot by shot from the broadcast video, using labeling tools I built: who hit each ball, what kind of shot it was, and where it landed on the court.

Computer vision models help find the ball, the players and the court. Their output is scored against my hand labels, not trusted on its own. See the pickleball project for how that works.

Only landings I placed by hand after seeing the bounce are on this page. Model estimates are left out.

A ball that landed out is drawn where it landed and counts in the total, but in no lane and no depth band.
