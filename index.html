★ Flagship

Pickleball Analytics — Computer Vision Rally Tracking

January 2026 – Present · Active Development

3 YOLO11 models, one decode pass F1 0.60 bounce detection 155-clip hand-labeled truth set Honest amber "CLOSE" line calls

Problem: Sports like tennis have years of data and models that track player and ball movement. Pickleball doesn't have that yet — and the sport is growing fast.

Approach: An 8-stage pipeline that turns one camera angle of game footage into an annotated rally video and a full stat sheet: ball tracking (Kalman-filtered), player keypoints, court homography, bounce/hit events, shot classification, and automated line calls.

Result: Measured precision/recall against hand-labeled ground truth, tracked across every release — and a line-calling design that reports an honest "CLOSE" when measurement uncertainty exceeds the ball's radius, instead of a confident wrong answer.

Key takeaway: the statistician's instinct — quantify your uncertainty and never overclaim — applied to a computer vision system.

Approach

How I Work with Data

Every project starts with a question and ends with a clear answer someone can act on.

01

Ask the Right Question

In plain English: what are we trying to answer? For example — how can game footage help pickleball players understand and improve their game?

02

Understand the Data

How was it collected? What assumptions come with it? Explore every column, visualize to spot issues, and know what's trustworthy before modeling.

03

Model & Validate

Fit the right model for the question — not the fanciest one. Check assumptions, measure performance, and ask: does this answer the original question?

04

Communicate Clearly

What visuals and language help someone who isn't a statistician act on the findings? Insight only matters if people can use it.

AI-assisted, statistician-verified

I use AI development tools as force multipliers — they make me faster at building. But every model I ship is validated the old-fashioned way: hand-labeled ground truth, measured precision and recall, calibration checks, and honest reporting of what doesn't work yet. That combination — modern AI tooling with statistical rigor — is exactly what I bring to teams adopting AI.
