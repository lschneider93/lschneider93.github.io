Cancer and genetic testing research

Myriad Genetics · Biostatistician · Jul 2022 – Oct 2025

Research questions

  • Do patients with a known cancer type score differently on our tests than patients without cancer, and can we measure that difference?
  • Can we measure the effect of educating healthcare providers on genetic testing rates?
  • Given an assumed hazard ratio of 1.2 to 1.5, how many patients does a study need to reach 80 to 95% power?

What I did

  • Fit survival, logistic, and mixed-effects models
  • Built R workflows with data quality checks and graphics that helped teams understand the data
  • Reviewed other statisticians' analyses and reproduced their results

Result

Co-authored a 2025 study in Obstetrics and Gynecology: online screening and virtual education took completed genetic testing from about 1 in 6 to 1 in 3 eligible patients.
  • R
  • Survival analysis
  • Logistic regression
  • Mixed-effects models
  • Power and sample size
  • Data quality

Pickleball analytics

Personal project · Computer vision and sports statistics · Jan 2026 – Present

Research question

  • Can gameplay footage be turned into feedback a player can act on, the way a coach would?

What I built

  • Computer vision models that find the ball, the players, and the court in every frame
  • Labeling tools and a shot-by-shot record of pro matches: who hit it, from where, and where it landed
  • A scorecard that checks every model against hand-labeled answers

Result

Rallies are found automatically 96% of the time (167 of 174). The court is located within one foot on 97% of checks. Bounce detection is the weak stage, and the scorecard says so.
  • Python
  • Computer vision
  • Model evaluation
  • Object tracking
  • Data labeling
  • Data visualization

Estimating the water stored in Utah's snowpack

M.S. thesis · Utah State University · 2021 – 2022

Research question

  • Can combining two data sources improve estimates of the water stored in Utah's snow?

What I built

  • rsnodas, a published R package that downloads both data sources, fits a local model to the snow stations, and blends it with the national grid
  • A 10-fold cross-validation of all three approaches, year by year

Result

The blend was about as accurate as the national grid and more stable: it avoided the error spikes of 2014 and 2022. The gain was small, and I reported it that way.
  • R
  • Spatial modeling
  • Generalized additive models
  • Cross-validation
  • R package development

Utah's rivers, ranked against history

Personal project · Live dashboard · 2026

Research question

  • Is this river high or low today, compared with every past year on this date?

What I built

  • A dashboard that pulls live USGS readings in your browser, with no server behind it
  • A percentile for every Utah streamgage against its own record, plus a Great Salt Lake level tracker
  • Data quality notes that show stale, missing, and placeholder readings instead of hiding them

Result

Every active Utah gage is ranked live, with records up to 131 years deep.
  • JavaScript
  • Public APIs
  • Percentiles
  • Data quality
  • Maps
  • Data visualization

A year of Citi Bike, checked before charted

Personal project · Data pipeline and dashboard · 2026

Research question

  • What does a year of New York bike trips show once every row has been checked?

What I built

  • A DuckDB pipeline over 45.6 million trips, with a written rule for every row removed
  • Charts of daily demand, rush hours for members and casual riders, trip length, and busiest stations

Result

45.4 million trips kept and 0.37% removed, each for a stated reason. One day with zero trips turned out to be real: a blizzard shut the system down.
  • Python
  • SQL (DuckDB)
  • Data pipelines
  • Data quality
  • JavaScript
  • Data visualization

My Approach

How I work through a question, on every project.

01

Clear Question

I start by putting the question in plain English.

02

Explore the Data

I investigate each column, build graphics and simple summaries, and get a clear picture of what I'm working with.

03

Model Selection

I find the model that fits the question and the data assumptions.

04

Validate and Explain

I check that results hold up, then write down what I learned, what worked, and what didn't.

05

Next Steps

I note the new questions that came up, decide what to investigate next, and repeat the process.
